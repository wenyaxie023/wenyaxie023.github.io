I’m **Wenya Xie**, a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the **University of Minnesota (UMN)**, advised by [**Prof. Zirui Liu**](https://zirui-ray-liu.github.io/). Previously, I worked as a research assistant at CUHK (Shenzhen) with Prof. Benyou Wang and Feng Jiang. I received my bachelor’s degree from Wuhan University.

I’m excited by how the way people work with AI is changing. As AI moves from answering one-off prompts to acting on our behalf, people rarely spell out everything they want, so models have to understand the person they work with: what a vague request means, what the person prefers, and how that changes over time. I work on building this ability across the model lifecycle:

- **Training.** I’m currently working on mid-training with synthetic data to help models predict how individuals decide, and on post-training from real-world user feedback that is vague and underspecified.
- **Test time.** [How Memory Management Impacts LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16067) (ACL 2026 Oral) shows that retrieving a similar past task can lead an agent to repeat an earlier mistake. [Reasoning While Asking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22139) (ACL 2026) teaches reasoning models to ask clarifying questions when information is missing. [Word Salad Chopper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00536) (EMNLP 2025 Oral) detects and cuts repetitive reasoning during decoding. At Adobe, I built AJO Orchestration Guard to make agent skills and workflows execute more accurately on underspecified requests.
- **Memory and evaluation.** [DynamicMem](https://wenyaxie023.github.io/DynamicMem/) tests whether assistants can track a user’s changing habits and preferences from activity across apps. I’ve also studied reproducibility in reasoning evaluation ([FP32](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09501), NeurIPS 2025 Oral).

I’m always excited to connect. Questions, ideas, and early-stage thoughts are all welcome.
If you have anything you'd like to discuss, feel free to reach out!
